\begin{abstract}
EV charging usability depends on multiple factors, including connector compatibility, operating hours, access conditions, and network information. This project compares existing charging infrastructure data, interoperability protocols, and semantic models to identify differences and gaps in how these factors are represented. Based on this comparison, we design PluggedIn, a data model centered on vehicle-specific practical usability. PluggedIn integrates these factors while explicitly representing adapter compatibility, driver network membership, and structured operating periods, providing a foundation for future knowledge graph representation. The project repository, data models, and related artifacts are available at \url{https://github.com/chohoon901/PluggedIn}.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{EV charging, relational data modeling, SQLite, adapter compatibility, data accessibility}

\section{Problem Definition}
Our goal is to let drivers and fleet managers retrieve station availability, connector compatibility, operating hours, and network payment conditions. Flat station and infrastructure lists lose vehicle-to-port suitability associations, adapter requirements, and membership constraints. 

We therefore link each charging station to structured operating periods, individual port blocks, standardized connector types, and organizational roles. Furthermore, we explicitly associate drivers with network memberships and evaluate vehicle charging feasibility through direct connections or adapter-mediated compatibility. 

Our database must answer critical operational questions: \textit{``Can a specific vehicle charge at this station, either directly or via an adapter, and is it currently open and accessible under the driver's network membership?''} State your domain, intended users, and the questions your database must answer. \section{Proposed Relational Model}
\subsection{Station, Organization, and Core Entity Relations}
\textbf{Station} records spatial coordinates, address details (street, city, province), truck parking availability, maximum vehicle class restrictions, access restrictions, guest payment permissions, and operational status. \textbf{Organization} describes reusable organizational entities, distinguishing site owners, network operators, and charging networks through an ISA-equivalent relational pattern. Business identifiers and station names serve as mandatory candidate keys. Every relation has an autogenerated integer primary key named \texttt{Id}. The complete relation list appears on the final page.

\subsection{Ports, Operating Periods, and Adapter Relations}
\textbf{OperatingPeriod} records temporal availability by isolating opening and closing times per day, replacing unstructured free-text hours. \textbf{Port} records target-specific outlets and references one station, capturing current types, power capacities, and quantities. \textbf{ConnectorType} standardizes plug nomenclature across disparate sources. To solve real-world connector mismatches, \textbf{Adapter} records hardware specifications, while \textbf{AdapterCompatibility} links adapters to plug-side and inter-side connector types.

\subsection{Vehicles, Drivers, and Membership Relations}
\textbf{Vehicle} stores specifications including VIN, licence plate, make, model, class, range, and maximum charging power. \textbf{Driver} captures user details and commercial carrier affiliations. The many-to-many relationship between vehicles and supported connectors becomes \textbf{VehicleConnector}. Similarly, the many-to-many relationship linking drivers to charging networks becomes \textbf{MemberOf}, capturing validity start/end dates and associated payment methods with a unique pair of foreign keys.

\subsection{Relationship Mapping and Integrity Constraints}
One-to-many relationships use foreign keys on the many side. Business candidate keys use both \texttt{UNIQUE} and \texttt{NOT NULL}. Allowed operational statuses and current types use \texttt{CHECK} constraints where applicable. Foreign-key enforcement must be enabled on every SQLite connection via \texttt{PRAGMA foreign\_keys = ON;}. Referenced records cannot be deleted while dependent rows remain; no cascade is assumed. 
Mapping assumptions. Organizations, stations, vehicles, and drivers may exist before their related transactional rows or memberships are entered; hence parent-side participation is optional. Every port and operating period must reference an existing station. Input validation enforces fixed ISO-style formats for temporal and spatial data.

\section{Relational Operations}
\subsection{Creating and Populating Relations}
The PluggedIn relational database is implemented in SQLite with 13 tables representing charging stations, vehicles, drivers, organizations, and their relationships. The schema defines primary keys, foreign keys, uniqueness constraints, and domain checks. Foreign-key enforcement is enabled using \texttt{PRAGMA foreign\_keys = ON}.

For example, consider the \textbf{Port} relation, which represents the charging ports belonging to a station. The following SQL statement creates the relation:

\begin{verbatim}
CREATE TABLE port (
    port_id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
    station_id INTEGER NOT NULL
        REFERENCES station(station_id)
        ON DELETE CASCADE,
    number SMALLINT NOT NULL CHECK(number > 0),
    connector_type_id INTEGER NOT NULL
        REFERENCES connector_type(connector_type_id),
    power_kw NUMERIC(6,1) CHECK(power_kw > 0),
    current_type VARCHAR(5)
        CHECK(current_type IN ('AC','DC')),
    quantity SMALLINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 1
        CHECK(quantity > 0),
    UNIQUE(station_id, number)
    );
\end{verbatim}

Here, \texttt{}{port\_id} serves as the primary key, while \texttt{station\_id} references the Station relation. The \texttt{UNIQUE(station\_id, number)} constraint ensures that port numbers are unique within each station, preserving the identifying relationship from the ERD. Additionally, \texttt{ON DELETE CASCADE} removes associated ports when their station is deleted.

To populate the database, sample records are inserted into the corresponding relations. The dataset includes five charging stations, their associated ports and operating periods, two drivers, one vehicle, and two network memberships. Before inserting new records, the system checks whether they already exist to prevent duplicate insertions and conflicting data.

\subsection{SELECT Queries}
Suppose we want to retrieve all charging ports belonging to a particular station. The following query selects the ports associated with Station 1 and sorts them in ascending order by \texttt{port\_id}.

\begin{verbatim}
SELECT *
FROM port
WHERE station_id = 1
ORDER BY port_id;
\end{verbatim}

Another example is retrieving organizations that serve as charging networks. Since \textbf{Network} is a subclass of \textbf{Organization}, the two relations can be joined using \texttt{organization\_id}.

\begin{verbatim}
SELECT o.*
FROM organization AS o
JOIN network AS n
    ON o.organization_id = n.organization_id
ORDER BY o.organization_id;
\end{verbatim}

This query retrieves only organizations with a corresponding record in the Network relation, demonstrating how an ISA relationship can be queried through a relational JOIN.

\subsection{INSERT, UPDATE, and DELETE Operations}
A vehicle is registered with a supported connector type. The following statement inserts a new association into the \textbf{VehicleAccepts} relation:

\begin{verbatim}
INSERT INTO vehicle_accepts
    (vehicle_id, connector_type_id)
VALUES (1, 1);
\end{verbatim}

If the vehicle's accepted connector types change, the existing associations can be deleted and replaced with the updated set.

\begin{verbatim}
DELETE FROM vehicle_accepts
WHERE vehicle_id = 1;

INSERT INTO vehicle_accepts
    (vehicle_id, connector_type_id)
VALUES (1, 2);
\end{verbatim}

This approach updates the many-to-many relationship between \textbf{Vehicle} and \textbf{ConnectorType} without deleting the vehicle itself. At least one connector type must be selected to maintain a valid set of vehicle-connector associations.

Other attributes can be modified using an \texttt{UPDATE} statement. For example, the following query changes the charging power of \texttt{Port 1}:

\begin{verbatim}
UPDATE port
SET power_kw = 120.0
WHERE port_id = 1;
\end{verbatim}

The \texttt{WHERE} clause ensures that only the specified port is modified. Foreign-key and domain constraints help maintain data integrity during these operations.


\section{Architecture and User Interface}

PluggedIn is a server-rendered FastAPI application using local SQLite. The presentation (\texttt{pl}), domain (\texttt{cmn}), and data access (\texttt{dal}) layers are connected in \texttt{main.py}. CRUD operations for drivers, vehicles, and memberships, station searches, and detail pages work without JavaScript. An external API supplies the map.

The \texttt{cmn} layer validates input and evaluates opening hours, access, connector compatibility, and payment as \textbf{Pass/Fail/Unknown}, producing an overall \textbf{Available/Unavailable/Needs Review} result. The \texttt{dal} layer uses SQLAlchemy ORM and bound parameters; a unit of work per use case commits or rolls back related database changes together. Successful form submissions redirect with HTTP 303; invalid submissions return an HTML form with HTTP 400, preserving all submitted values. API duplicate-key errors return HTTP 409.

\section{Reproducibility and Evaluation}

The application uses Python 3.12, FastAPI 0.162.0, and SQLAlchemy 2.0.55. \path{dal/scripts/schema.sql} defines 13 tables and 65 columns. Section 3 describes the main queries; \path{dal/repositories/} contains ORM implementations, and \path{README.md} provides setup instructions.

In a virtual environment, install the dependencies from \path{requirements-dev.txt} and the project, run \texttt{init\_db}, \texttt{seed\_demo}, and \texttt{validate\_db}, then start uvicorn. UNIQUE constraints reject duplicate driver licence numbers, VINs, and station coordinates. Foreign keys reject invalid references and deletion of connector types in use. Station fields \texttt{network\_id}, \texttt{owner\_id}, and \texttt{operator\_id} allow NULL. Deleting a network cascades to memberships, but referencing stations may prevent deletion.

The repository records 170 passing tests, including browser tests, and 91 subtests on 27 October 2026. Team-authored code uses the MIT License; bundled Leaflet 1.9.4 uses BSD 2-Clause, with its full license text included in the repository.

\section*{Relations and Constraints}
\label{sec:relations-constraints}
Underlining denotes a primary key (PK). CK denotes an additional candidate key enforced by \texttt{UNIQUE} and \texttt{NOT NULL}. PKs are generated integers, except for organization subtypes, which share the superclass key. All PK/CK attributes are mandatory. Other attributes are nullable unless marked mandatory below.

\begin{enumerate}
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{(\arabic{enumi})}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}

\item \textbf{organization} (\underline{organization\_id}, name, website).\\
CK: \{name\}. Specialization is overlapping and total: each organization has at least one role.

\item \textbf{site\_owner} (\underline{organization\_id}, business\_type).\\
PK/FK: organization\_id references organization.organization\_id, mandatory. No additional CK.

\item \textbf{operator} (\underline{organization\_id}, support\_phone).\\
PK/FK: organization\_id references organization.organization\_id, mandatory. No additional CK.

\item \textbf{network} (\underline{organization\_id}, payment\_method).\\
PK/FK: organization\_id references organization.organization\_id, mandatory. No additional CK.

\item \textbf{station} (\underline{station\_id}, latitude, longitude, name, street, city, province, status, access\_restriction, accepts\_guest\_payment, max\_vehicle\_class, has\_truck\_parking, owner\_id, operator\_id, network\_id).\\
CK: \{latitude, longitude\}. Latitude is between -90 and 90, and longitude between -180 and 180. FK: owner\_id references site\_owner.organization\_id and operator\_id references operator.organization\_id, network\_id references network.organization\_id, all optional. Accepts\_guest\_payment and has\_truck\_parking are mandatory Booleans, both defaulting to \texttt{FALSE}.

\item \textbf{connector\_type} (\underline{connector\_type\_id}, name, max\_power\_kw).\\
CK: \{name\}. Max\_power\_kw must be positive when supplied.

\item \textbf{port} 
(\underline{port\_id}, station\_id, number, connector\_type\_id, power\_kw, current\_type, quantity).\\
CK: \{station\_id, number\}. FK: station\_id references station.station\_id and connector\_type\_id references connector\_type.connector\_type\_id, both mandatory. Number must be positive. Quantity is mandatory, positive, and defaults to 1. When supplied, power\_kw must be positive and current\_type must be \texttt{AC} or \texttt{DC}.

\item \textbf{operating\_period} (\underline{operating\_period\_id}, station\_id, day, open\_time, close\_time).\\
CK: \{station\_id, day, open\_time\}. FK: station\_id references station.station\_id, mandatory. All attributes are mandatory. Day is one of \texttt{Mon}, \texttt{Tue}, \texttt{Wed}, \texttt{Thu}, \texttt{Fri}, \texttt{Sat}, or \texttt{Sun}. Close\_time must be later than open\_time.

\item \textbf{adapter} (\underline{adapter\_id}, model, manufacturer, max\_power\_kw, from\_connector\_type\_id, to\_connector\_type\_id).\\
CK: \{model\}. Both connector attributes are mandatory FKs to connector\_type.connector\_type\_id. The two connector IDs must differ. Max\_power\_kw must be positive when supplied.

\item \textbf{vehicle} (\underline{vehicle\_id}, vin, licence\_plate, make, model, class, range\_km, max\_charge\_kw).\\
CK: \{vin\}. Range\_km and max\_charge\_kw must be positive when supplied.

\item \textbf{vehicle\_accepts} (\underline{vehicle\_accepts\_id}, vehicle\_id, connector\_type\_id).\\
CK: \{vehicle\_id, connector\_type\_id\}. FK: vehicle\_id references vehicle.vehicle\_id and connector\_type\_id references connector\_type.connector\_type\_id, both mandatory.

\item \textbf{driver} (\underline{driver\_id}, licence\_number, name, carrier\_name).\\
CK: \{licence\_number\}.

\item \textbf{membership} (\underline{membership\_id}, driver\_id, network\_id, start\_date, end\_date).\\
CK: \{driver\_id, network\_id, start\_date\}. FK: driver\_id references driver.driver\_id and network\_id references network.organization\_id, both mandatory. End\_date is NULL or on or after start\_date.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Participation and additional rules.}
The ERD requires at least one port and operating period per station, one station per owner and operator, and one supported connector type per vehicle. These minimum counts and total organization-role participation require application-level checks. Overlapping operating periods and memberships are not prohibited.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Referential actions.}
Cascade deletion applies from organizations to subtypes, stations to ports and operating periods, vehicles to vehicle\_accepts, and drivers or networks to memberships. Other FKs block deletion while dependent rows remain; these restrictions can also block a cascade.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Implementation boundary.}
SQLite foreign-key enforcement must be enabled on every connection. Optional numeric attributes allow NULL despite positivity checks. Rejecting empty mandatory text values requires additional validation.
